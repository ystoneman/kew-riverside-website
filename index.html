04 / Options for retaining provision

Where effort could matter most.

An editorial assessment of practical priorities. These are not measured probabilities of stopping closure.

How we ranked them

Priority reflects relevance to a closure decision, the strength of the argument if evidenced, and what can be done before a decision. School-specific feasibility is still unproven. Work on formal participation alongside the substantive case.

01
Highest priority

Build a costed plan to keep teaching on the site

A credible alternative gives decision-makers an implementable choice. Model a smaller intake, shared leadership, a federation or appropriate use of spare space, with a realistic staffing and funding plan.

Effort: HighNeeds school-specific budgets and partner commitments
First deliverable, limits & supporting records

First deliverable

Produce a three-year budget with low, central and high pupil scenarios. Identify who would deliver it and what support they would commit.

What this cannot establish

A federation or merger may preserve teaching on the site without preserving the current institution. Agree which outcome matters. No option is demonstrated viable by the records collected here.

Closing maintained schools: statutory guidance · School financial information · The school's private finance initiative arrangement

02
High priority

Test the local demand forecast

The borough trend is clear, but a borough average cannot settle the case for one school. Compare forecasts with actual admissions, local cohorts and dated housing-delivery assumptions.

Effort: Medium–highGood historical evidence; current Kew forecast missing
First deliverable, limits & supporting records

First deliverable

Request the latest Kew-area model, its inputs and forecast errors. Reconcile the 2023 strategy with the later treatment of housing; present credible delivery scenarios.

What this cannot establish

A historic forecast is not proof that a later forecast is wrong. Proposed homes do not automatically become pupils at Kew Riverside.

School Place Planning Strategy: the Kew area · School place planning annual update: October 2025 data

03
High priority

Audit the actual saving from closure

The useful comparison is the avoidable cost of keeping the school open against the full cost of closing it. Continuing building payments could change the balance.

Effort: Medium–highPFI arrangement documented; closure cost model missing
First deliverable, limits & supporting records

First deliverable

Seek an annual cash-flow comparison including private finance initiative obligations, transition costs, redundancy, transport and any site-use income. Separate costs that end from costs that continue.

What this cannot establish

The existence of a PFI contract does not prove that closure saves nothing. Its terms and the council's actual options must be established.

The school's private finance initiative arrangement · Council responsibilities for school buildings · School financial information

04
High priority

Document pupil impacts and receiving-school capacity

Decision-makers need practical evidence about each year group, additional support, safe journeys, accessibility, continuity and siblings. Aggregate empty places do not answer every family-level question.

Effort: MediumSchool-specific impact assessment missing
First deliverable, limits & supporting records

First deliverable

Request capacity by year group and provision for special educational needs and disabilities (SEND). Submit anonymised, specific examples with realistic mitigations and alternatives.

What this cannot establish

Impact evidence may improve a transition plan without preventing closure. Keep children's identities and individual needs out of public campaign material.

Closing maintained schools: statutory guidance · School place planning annual update: October 2025 data

05
Essential alongside 1–4

Put the evidence through every formal decision stage

A concise, referenced case is easier to assess than a signature count alone. Coordinate who addresses budgets, demand, alternatives and pupil impacts, while preserving each person's own views.

Effort: Low–mediumPublic participation rules available
First deliverable, limits & supporting records

First deliverable

Confirm the official submission channel and deadline. Submit evidence, seek acknowledgement, check the committee agenda, and register a speaker or a compliant petition where useful.

What this cannot establish

A petition is a route to consideration, not a veto. Public campaigning does not replace a response during the formal representation period.

How to speak or ask questions at council meetings · Petitions to Richmond Council · Closing maintained schools: statutory guidance

06
Conditional

Obtain a focused legal review if a concrete defect emerges

An education or public-law solicitor can assess a specific concern about the process, evidence or legal duties once the relevant documents are available.

Effort: High / professional costsDepends on the actual proposal and decision record
First deliverable, limits & supporting records

First deliverable

Provide the notice, consultation material, decision report and a precise chronology. Ask promptly about applicable deadlines and remedies.

What this cannot establish

Disagreement with the merits is not by itself a legal defect. Parents do not have a general direct right of appeal to the Schools Adjudicator for a community-school closure. This ranking is not legal advice.

Closing maintained schools: statutory guidance

What about publicity and fundraising?

Publicity can recruit expertise and make evidence easier to find. Fundraising can help pay for independent analysis or a credible transition. Neither establishes recurring financial viability by itself. Treat both as support for a documented plan, with clear accountability for any money raised.

About this collection

Evidence first.
Uncertainty visible.

This is an independent reference hub, created to help people understand the closure question and assess ways of retaining local school provision. It is not an official school or council publication.

Sources and coverage

Research prioritised the school, Richmond Council, Achieving for Children, Ofsted and the Department for Education. Sources were checked on 21 September 2026. Historical background reaches back to the school's opening through the first inspection report. The source library labels unread links explicitly. Searching these routes cannot establish that every relevant public record has been found.

Facts, statements and analysis

A source's account is attributed to its publisher. Forecasts retain their dates and are distinguished from observed data. The option ranking and suggested evidence requests are editorial analysis. They do not assert that an alternative has been approved, funded or proven feasible.

Updates and corrections

This is a dated research snapshot, not a live news feed. Check official notices before acting. A correction should identify the specific claim, the public source URL and document date, and explain the change. The downloadable source index provides stable record identifiers for that purpose. Send a correction or suggestion.

Privacy, accessibility and reuse

This site publishes short summaries and links to the original public records. It does not reproduce pupil photographs, private correspondence or individual family information. The evidence pages have no analytics or advertising scripts. The feedback form and reviewed suggestions board use separate submission and moderation services; see the privacy and moderation notice. Charts have text explanations and data tables. Publishers retain rights in their original documents.
