Before you respond.

Five quick answers to help you take the next step.

Full proposal, dates & who decides →
Has closure already been decided?

No. The council is consulting on a proposal. Its timetable plans a decision on whether to proceed in November 2026 and a final decision in April 2027. Closure on 1 September 2027 would depend on approval.

Council timetable, PDF page 7 · Dates and possible outcomes

Can families still apply for a place?

Yes. The council’s general FAQ says applications and admissions can continue during this initial, pre-statutory consultation. Normal admissions rules still apply; a place or the school’s future cannot be guaranteed. Check the latest school and council information before making plans.

Council FAQ, question 18, PDF page 8

What makes a useful consultation response?

Send your view through the official form by 16 October 2026. It asks for your reasons and offers space for alternatives and comments. Our suggestion: explain what matters to your family or community, use specific examples and link to evidence that others can check.

Official response form and deadline · Printable response checklist (PDF)

Does a letter or supporter entry here count as my council response?

No. These are contributions to this parent-led website, separate from the council consultation. Letters are not automatically forwarded. Submit your official response directly, even if you also share your experience or support here.

How community letters work · Respond directly to the council

Would raising £400,000 guarantee the school stays open?

No. The council forecasts a deficit of over £400,000 by 2028/29; that is not an agreed fundraising target. Funding would need to support a sustainable plan that decision-makers assess. This site is exploring that option and is not collecting donations or pledges.

Council financial forecast, PDF page 3 · Explore the funding questions

04 / Options for retaining provision

Where effort could matter most.

Seven options for retaining provision, including exploratory crowdfunding. These are not measured probabilities of stopping closure.

How we ranked them

The council has published reasons for not preferring the alternatives it considered; a costed comparison is still sought. Priority reflects relevance to a closure decision, the strength of the argument if evidenced, and what can be done before a decision. School-specific feasibility is still unproven. Crowdfunding follows the recovery plan because they are connected; its position does not establish feasibility. Work on formal participation alongside the substantive case.

01
Highest priority

Build a costed plan to keep teaching on the site

A credible alternative gives decision-makers an implementable choice. Model a smaller intake, shared leadership, a federation or appropriate use of spare space, with a realistic staffing and funding plan.

Effort: HighNeeds school-specific budgets and partner commitments
First deliverable, limits & supporting records

First deliverable

Produce a three-year budget with low, central and high pupil scenarios. Identify who would deliver it and what support they would commit.

What this cannot establish

A federation or merger may preserve teaching on the site without preserving the current institution. Agree which outcome matters. No option is demonstrated viable by the records collected here.

Closing maintained schools: statutory guidance · School financial information · The school's private finance initiative arrangement

02
Exploratory — feasibility to establish

Explore community crowdfunding

Could local residents, families and businesses help fund a viable future for Kew Riverside? Community fundraising could contribute to a costed recovery plan and give the school time to make changes.

The council projects a budget deficit of over £400,000 by 2028/29. That is a forecast, not an agreed fundraising target. We need to understand the annual funding gap, what would make the school sustainable afterwards, and whether a funded alternative would change the proposal.

Source: Council consultation, “Financial strain” and the consultation leaflet, rechecked 21 September 2026.

An option to explore. No fundraising campaign or pledge collection is being launched here. This is Yann’s parent-led initiative; no school, PTA or council endorsement is implied.

Effort: HighNeeds a recovery budget, donor interest and an agreed recipient
First deliverable, limits & supporting records

First deliverable

Establish the funding needed for an agreed recovery period, identify an organisation able to receive and manage contributions, and seek a written assessment from the school and council of how a funded plan would affect the proposal. Assess donor interest before deciding whether to launch a public appeal.

What this cannot establish

A one-off donation does not resolve a continuing annual shortfall. Fundraising would need a credible plan for pupil demand, costs and future income. Community wealth does not establish donor commitments, and raising money would not itself secure a decision to keep the school open.

This sits beside the costed recovery plan because the two are connected. Its position does not indicate an established likelihood of success.

03
High priority

Test the local demand forecast

The borough trend is clear, but a borough average cannot settle the case for one school. Compare forecasts with actual admissions, local cohorts and dated housing-delivery assumptions.

Effort: Medium–highSchool and local forecasts published; assumptions need scrutiny
First deliverable, limits & supporting records

First deliverable

Test the published school and Kew-area forecasts against their inputs and past errors. Reconcile dated housing assumptions and examine credible delivery scenarios.

What this cannot establish

A historic forecast is not proof that a later forecast is wrong. Proposed homes do not automatically become pupils at Kew Riverside.

School Place Planning Strategy: the Kew area · School place planning annual update: October 2025 data

04
High priority

Audit the actual saving from closure

The useful comparison is the avoidable cost of keeping the school open against the full cost of closing it. Continuing building payments could change the balance.

Effort: Medium–highPFI arrangement documented; closure cost model missing
First deliverable, limits & supporting records

First deliverable

Seek an annual cash-flow comparison including private finance initiative obligations, transition costs, redundancy, transport and any site-use income. Separate costs that end from costs that continue.

What this cannot establish

The existence of a PFI contract does not prove that closure saves nothing. Its terms and the council's actual options must be established.

The school's private finance initiative arrangement · Council responsibilities for school buildings · School financial information

05
High priority

Document pupil impacts and receiving-school capacity

Decision-makers need practical evidence about each year group, additional support, safe journeys, accessibility, continuity and siblings. Aggregate empty places do not answer every family-level question.

Effort: MediumTransition commitments published; detailed impact evidence still sought
First deliverable, limits & supporting records

First deliverable

Request capacity by year group and provision for special educational needs and disabilities (SEND). Submit anonymised, specific examples with realistic mitigations and alternatives.

What this cannot establish

Impact evidence may improve a transition plan without preventing closure. Keep children's identities and individual needs out of public campaign material.

Closing maintained schools: statutory guidance · School place planning annual update: October 2025 data

06
Essential alongside 1–5

Put the evidence through every formal decision stage

A concise, referenced case is easier to assess than a signature count alone. Coordinate who addresses budgets, demand, alternatives and pupil impacts, while preserving each person's own views.

Effort: Low–mediumPublic participation rules available
First deliverable, limits & supporting records

First deliverable

Respond through the official council-linked form by its stated date, 16 October 2026. Keep a copy, check later committee agendas and respond again during any statutory representation period.

What this cannot establish

A petition is a route to consideration, not a veto. Public campaigning does not replace a response during the formal representation period.

How to speak or ask questions at council meetings · Petitions to Richmond Council · Closing maintained schools: statutory guidance

07
Conditional

Obtain a focused legal review if a concrete defect emerges

An education or public-law solicitor can assess a specific concern about the process, evidence or legal duties once the relevant documents are available.

Effort: High / professional costsDepends on the actual proposal and decision record
First deliverable, limits & supporting records

First deliverable

Provide the notice, consultation material, decision report and a precise chronology. Ask promptly about applicable deadlines and remedies.

What this cannot establish

Disagreement with the merits is not by itself a legal defect. Parents do not have a general direct right of appeal to the Schools Adjudicator for a community-school closure. This ranking is not legal advice.

This is Yann Stoneman’s initiative as a Kew Riverside parent to help keep the school open. The evidence supports scrutiny of the proposal and alternatives. The school and council do not operate this site. Read my position, responsibilities and family story.

Sources and coverage

Yann Stoneman is responsible for updates and corrections. Research prioritised the school, Richmond Council, Achieving for Children, Ofsted and the Department for Education. Sources were checked on 21 September 2026. Historical background reaches back to the school's opening through the first inspection report. The source library labels unread links explicitly. Searching these routes cannot establish that every relevant public record has been found.

Facts, statements and analysis

A source's account is attributed to its publisher. Forecasts retain their dates and are distinguished from observed data. The option ranking and suggested evidence requests are editorial analysis. They do not assert that an alternative has been approved, funded or proven feasible.

Updates and corrections

This is a dated research snapshot, not a live news feed. Read the update history. Check official notices before acting. A correction should identify the specific claim, the public source URL and document date, and explain the change. The downloadable source index provides stable record identifiers for that purpose. Request a private correction or suggest an improvement.

Privacy, accessibility and reuse

This site publishes short summaries and links to the original public records. It does not reproduce pupil photographs, private correspondence or individual family information. The evidence pages have no analytics or advertising scripts. The feedback form and reviewed suggestions board and community letters use separate submission and moderation services; see the privacy and moderation notice. Charts have text explanations and data tables. Publishers retain rights in their original documents.
