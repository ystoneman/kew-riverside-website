01
Highest priority

Build a costed plan to keep teaching on the site

A budget and delivery commitments would turn a possible alternative into something the council can assess.

First useful step Ask for a three-year budget with low, central and high pupil scenarios, plus a named delivery organisation and its commitments.

Keep in mind Keeping teaching on the site may change the institution or governance. A legal route alone does not establish a viable plan.

What a three-year plan needs

A credible alternative gives decision-makers an implementable choice. Model a smaller intake, shared leadership, a federation or appropriate use of spare space, with a realistic staffing and funding plan.

The leaflet says amalgamation would require a new free school. Current guidance also describes altering a surviving maintained school and, subject to conditions, replacing maintained schools with a new maintained school. Which routes could retain teaching at Kew, and what would each cost? Legal routes alone do not establish a partner, affordable plan or usable site. Council leaflet · Prescribed alterations guidance · Opening new schools guidance.

Effort: HighNeeds school-specific budgets and partner commitments

First deliverable

Produce a three-year budget with low, central and high pupil scenarios. Identify who would deliver it and what support they would commit.

What this cannot establish

A federation or merger may preserve teaching on the site without preserving the current institution. An alteration to a surviving maintained school and a replacement maintained school are different legal routes, with different procedures and conditions. The September 2026 guidance includes transitional rules for earlier proposals. Agree which outcome matters, identify the body willing to deliver it, and test premises, timing and costs. No option is demonstrated viable by the records collected here.

02
Exploratory — feasibility to establish

Explore community crowdfunding

Community funding could support a costed recovery period, if there is a credible future beyond it.

The council projects an accumulated budget deficit of over £400,000 by 2028/29. That is a forecast, not an agreed fundraising target. We need to understand the annual funding gap, what would make the school sustainable afterwards, and whether a funded alternative would change the proposal.

Source: Council consultation, “Financial strain” and the consultation leaflet, rechecked 21 September 2026.

An option to explore. No donations or pledges are collected here, and no recipient is agreed. This is Yann’s parent-led initiative; no school, PTA or council endorsement is implied.

First useful step Establish the recovery budget, a suitable recipient and how the school and council would assess a funded alternative.

Keep in mind A one-off donation does not resolve a continuing shortfall or secure a decision to keep the school open.

How to assess a funding proposal

Could local residents, families and businesses help fund a viable future for Kew Riverside? Community fundraising could contribute to a costed recovery plan and give the school time to make changes.

Effort: HighNeeds a recovery budget, donor interest and an agreed recipient

First deliverable

Establish the funding needed for an agreed recovery period, identify an organisation able to receive and manage contributions, and seek a written assessment from the school and council of how a funded plan would affect the proposal. Assess donor interest before deciding whether to launch a public appeal.

What this cannot establish

A one-off donation does not resolve a continuing annual shortfall. Fundraising would need a credible plan for pupil demand, costs and future income. Community wealth does not establish donor commitments, and raising money would not itself secure a decision to keep the school open.

This sits beside the costed recovery plan because the two are connected. Its position does not indicate an established likelihood of success.

03
High priority

Test the local demand forecast

Check which school and local assumptions underpin the forecast. See the matched forecast check.

First useful step Compare forecasts with actual pupils for the same year and area, then ask which cohort and housing assumptions changed.

Keep in mind A historic forecast is not proof that a later one is wrong. Proposed homes do not automatically become Kew Riverside pupils.

How to check the forecast

Falling borough demand matters, but borough totals cannot settle the case for one school. An earlier Kew planning-area forecast was close to the 2025/26 total; later forecasts changed. See the matched forecast check, then ask which cohort and housing assumptions changed.

Effort: Medium–highSchool and local forecasts published; assumptions need scrutiny

First deliverable

Test each published school and Kew-area forecast against actuals for the same period and geography. Compare versions and explain changed pupil yields, housing completion dates and credible delivery scenarios. A planning permission is not an occupied family home.

What this cannot establish

A historic forecast is not proof that a later forecast is wrong. Proposed homes do not automatically become pupils at Kew Riverside.

04
An idea to develop with the school

Raise awareness to boost enrolment

Help families discover the school through a recruitment effort developed with the school and tested against its budget.

Applications and admissions can continue during the initial consultation under normal rules. Closure is proposed. An enquiry confirms neither a visit nor a place; the school’s future is not guaranteed. Council FAQ, question 18.

First useful step Agree a small recruitment pilot with the school: available places, an owner, costs and the sustained pupil increase the budget would need.

Keep in mind Enquiries, visits, applications and enrolments are different stages. No additional-pupil target is agreed to secure the school’s future.

How a school-coordinated pilot could work

Help local families discover the school through nurseries, parent groups, community notices, word of mouth and social media. Give them verified school information and a direct route to enquire about a visit; assess any sustained increase in enrolment against a costed recovery plan.

Suggested channels for a future campaign

  • Nurseries
  • Parent groups
  • Community notices
  • Word of mouth
  • Social media
Effort: Medium–highNeeds available places, school coordination and a recovery budget

A practical first step

Agree a small, time-limited recruitment pilot with the school. Establish available places by year group, what recruitment has already been tried, who would run the campaign and what budget or support it needs. Ask the school and council what sustained increase, by when, could materially change the recovery plan.

Give attention somewhere useful to go

Start with adult parents explaining why they chose the school, shared with their permission, and school-approved footage. Combine local parent groups, nurseries, community noticeboards and word of mouth with social media. Permission to publish a video on YouTube does not authorise reuse on other platforms. Reach families who could realistically attend, with a clear route to ask the school about a visit.

  1. 01Enquiries
  2. 02School visits
  3. 03Applications
  4. 04Enrolments

Ask the school for aggregate results at each step, alongside campaign costs. Check the effect of any additional pupils on staffing, funding and the budget. Keep prospective families’ and children’s details with the school and the official admissions service.

What this cannot establish

Going viral is possible, but cannot be promised. Views, enquiries and applications do not establish new enrolments or financial viability. A campaign may increase this school’s share of demand while the wider area’s child population falls. There is no agreed “extra pupils” target that guarantees the school stays open.

The council’s FAQ says applications and admissions can continue during the current pre-statutory consultation. Ask the school what visits are currently available; an enquiry does not confirm an appointment or an admission. Be open about the closure proposal: normal admissions arrangements apply, and neither a place nor the school’s future is guaranteed.

05
High priority

Audit the actual saving from closure

Compare the avoidable cost of keeping teaching with the full cost of closing it. Read the cost distinction.

First useful step Seek an annual comparison of costs that stop, continue or arise under closure and a realistic retained-school plan.

Keep in mind A continuing PFI contract does not prove closure saves nothing. Its terms and the council’s actual options need checking.

Which costs need comparing

The useful comparison is the avoidable cost of keeping the school open against the full cost of closing it. Continuing building payments could change the balance. See what the published costs do and do not establish.

Effort: Medium–highPFI arrangement documented; closure cost model missing

First deliverable

Seek an annual cash-flow comparison including private finance initiative obligations, transition costs, redundancy, transport and any site-use income. Separate costs that end from costs that continue.

What this cannot establish

The existence of a PFI contract does not prove that closure saves nothing. Its terms and the council's actual options must be established.

06
High priority

Document pupil impacts and receiving-school capacity

Empty places alone do not show how a move would work for each child. See the remaining questions.

First useful step Ask for staffed places by year group, the Kew-specific equality assessment, SEND support, safe journeys and funded transition arrangements.

Keep in mind Impact evidence may improve transition without preventing closure. Keep children’s identities and individual needs out of public material.

What pupil-impact evidence is needed

Decision-makers need practical evidence about each year group, additional support, safe journeys, accessibility, continuity and siblings. The borough equality assessment promises school-specific work, but a Kew assessment was not located in the reviewed public pack. See the remaining questions. Aggregate empty places do not answer every family-level question.

Effort: MediumTransition commitments published; detailed impact evidence still sought

First deliverable

Request staffed capacity by year group, the Kew-specific equality assessment, provision for special educational needs and disabilities (SEND), travel assessment and funded transition support. Submit anonymised, specific examples with realistic mitigations and alternatives.

What this cannot establish

Impact evidence may improve a transition plan without preventing closure. Keep children's identities and individual needs out of public campaign material.

07
Essential alongside 1–6

Put the evidence through every formal decision stage

Give decision-makers a concise, referenced case in your own words.

First useful step Respond through the official council-linked form by its stated date, 16 October 2026. Keep a copy and check any later representation period.

Keep in mind Do this alongside the other work. A community letter, petition or meeting attendance does not replace your official response.

Formal participation routes and records

A concise, referenced case is easier to assess than a signature count alone. Coordinate who addresses budgets, demand, alternatives and pupil impacts, while preserving each person's own views.

Effort: Low–mediumPublic participation rules available

First deliverable

Respond through the official council-linked form by its stated date, 16 October 2026. Keep a copy, check later committee agendas and respond again during any statutory representation period.

What this cannot establish

A petition is a route to consideration, not a veto. Public campaigning does not replace a response during the formal representation period.

08
Conditional

Obtain a focused legal review if a concrete defect emerges

A specialist can assess a specific concern about the process, evidence or legal duties.

First useful step Give a specialist solicitor the relevant documents and a precise concern; ask promptly about deadlines and possible remedies.

Keep in mind Disagreement alone is not a legal defect. Parents have no general direct adjudicator appeal for a community-school closure.

What a focused legal review would need

An education or public-law solicitor can assess a specific concern about the process, evidence or legal duties once the relevant documents are available.

Effort: High / professional costsDepends on the actual proposal and decision record

First deliverable

Provide the notice, consultation material, decision report and a precise chronology. Ask promptly about applicable deadlines and remedies.

What this cannot establish

Disagreement with the merits is not by itself a legal defect. Parents do not have a general direct right of appeal to the Schools Adjudicator for a community-school closure. This ranking is not legal advice.
