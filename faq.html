Ways to help

Choose the right route for your message.

What makes a useful consultation response?

Use the official response form and explain your view in your own words. It asks for reasons and offers space for alternatives and comments.

Prepare with other parents and use the meeting’s answers to strengthen your response. You can submit now if you are ready; do not miss 16 October while waiting for more information.

Our suggestion: explain what matters to your family or community, give specific examples and link to evidence others can check. Separate your experience from claims about budgets or demand. The printable checklist (PDF) can help you prepare.

Sources: Official response form

Does a community letter count as my council response?

No. This parent-led site is separate from the official consultation. Community letters are not automatically forwarded. Council sharing has its own optional consent and human review, and sending or counting a letter is not guaranteed.

Read or write a community letter to share your experience here, and respond directly to the council as well. Named supporters are also separate from official responses.

Sources: Community letters and sharing choices · Respond directly to the council

How can I share evidence, ideas or a meeting question?

Use Share ideas for an idea, a public source, a correction or a question for the meeting. It is not a donation form. Your submission goes privately to Yann for review; a name and reply email are optional.

Evidence and meeting questions need human assessment. Nothing is automatically published or sent to the council or meeting. Optional permission lets eligible suggestions be considered for the public board. For your personal views about the school’s future, write a community letter instead.

Please share links to public documents, not private school records or children’s personal information.

Dates & decisions

The next opportunities to take part, without treating a proposal as a final decision.

Has closure already been decided?

No. The council is consulting on a proposal. Its timetable places committee consideration of whether to proceed in November 2026 and a final decision in April 2027. Closure effective 1 September 2027 depends on approval.

The future timeline separates confirmed opportunities to take part from planned, conditional stages. November permission to continue would not itself approve closure.

Sources: Council timetable, PDF page 7

When is the meeting at the school?

29 September 2026 at 3.30pm, at Kew Riverside Primary School. The council leaflet lists this as a public engagement meeting. Check the council’s latest information before attending.

See the PTA preparation sessions and plan your questions with other parents.

You can suggest a question for Yann to consider. This does not submit it to the council or guarantee it will be asked. Raise time-sensitive questions through the official channels too.

When must we respond to the consultation?

The official response form states 16 October 2026. Submit directly through that form. It does not specify a closing time; allow time rather than assume a late-evening deadline.

The public leaflet gives a broader initial period, while the form provides this exact date. We explain the discrepancy in the timeline’s source note. A possible statutory notice would bring a separate four-week representation period later.

The money, in plain English

Distinguishing a forecast from a current debt or a fundraising target.

Is the £400,000 deficit annual or overall?

The council projects a deficit of more than £400,000 by 2028/29. It says reserves will be fully used by the end of the 2026/27 financial year, after which the school would begin accumulating a deficit.

That wording indicates a deficit building up over time; it does not describe £400,000 as the loss every year. The leaflet does not reproduce the annual budget figures, so the exact year-by-year path still needs checking.

Evidence to request: each year’s opening reserves, income, expenditure, in-year balance and closing cumulative balance, with the assumptions behind them.

Sources: Council financial forecast, PDF page 3

Link to this answer ↗
How long did it take to reach £400,000?

The leaflet does not say it has already reached that amount. It describes a future forecast: reserves exhausted at the end of 2026/27, then a deficit of over £400,000 by 2028/29, in a three-year budget plan.

The underlying plan is not included, so we cannot say exactly how the deficit would build up or divide the headline figure into an annual loss. The 2026/27 financial year ends on 31 March 2027; that is different from the end of the school year.

Sources: Council financial forecast, PDF page 3 · DfE financial-year guidance

Link to this answer ↗
Would raising £400,000 keep the school open?

There is no agreed £400,000 fundraising target or guarantee that raising it would prevent closure. The forecast is one part of a wider decision about demand, costs and provision.

Any funding option needs a sustainable, costed plan and agreement on who would receive and manage money. This site is exploring those questions; it collects no donations or pledges. “Share ideas” is a submissions form, not a payment button.

Your child’s school place

What is known now, and what families still need the council to explain.

Can families still apply to Kew Riverside?

Yes, applications and admissions can continue during this initial, pre-statutory consultation. Normal admissions rules still apply. Neither a place nor the school’s long-term future can be guaranteed.

Prospective families can ask the school about a visit and should check the latest school and council information before making plans.

Sources: Council FAQ, question 18, PDF page 8

Link to this answer ↗
Will Richmond Council just put us in a different school?

The published offer is a guaranteed Darell place for current Kew Riverside pupils whose parents want one, if closure goes ahead. Families can also apply to other schools where places are available.

The papers do not say every child will automatically be enrolled at Darell or that parents must accept it. They do not yet explain the guarantee’s application and acceptance steps. A guarantee is not the same as a completed offer; ask the council what action your family needs to take.

Sources: Kew leaflet, PDF page 2 · Council FAQ, question 17, PDF page 8

Link to this answer ↗
If closure is approved, when would we need to arrange another school?

Closure has not been decided. There is still an opportunity to make the case for keeping Kew Riverside open. See what parents can do together now.

No specific deadline for current families to choose or accept a replacement place has been published in the closure papers. The council says it would support families with admissions after a possible April 2027 decision, with alternative places arranged before September. Follow the school’s and Achieving for Children’s direct instructions when issued.

That is separate from normal admissions: families applying for Reception or transferring to secondary school should still follow those deadlines. Children with an Education, Health and Care Plan have a separate placement process. Do not assume everyone can wait until April or that no application will be needed.

Still to ask: exactly when current families must express preferences, how Darell offers will be made and accepted, and how individual support needs will be handled.

Sources: Council FAQ, question 29, PDF page 11 · Richmond’s 2027/28 admissions guide (school-hosted PDF)
