The proposal.

01 / The published timetable

A proposal has several decision points.

The consultation is a chance to give evidence and suggest alternatives. Proceeding to a statutory proposal would be a further step; it would not itself be approval to close the school.

  1. CURRENT · Responses due 16 October 2026

    Initial consultation

    Share your view, reasons and alternatives through the official response form. The leaflet also advertises a school engagement event on 29 September at 3.30pm; check the council page before attending.

    Response form · Leaflet, PDF page 8

  2. PLANNED · November 2026

    Decision on proceeding

    The leaflet schedules committee consideration of whether to proceed to statutory consultation. The calendar lists a meeting on 12 November, but the agenda has not yet been published, so no Kew-specific item is confirmed.

    Leaflet timetable · November meeting page

  3. CONDITIONAL · January–March 2027

    Formal proposal and representations

    If the process proceeds, the leaflet plans statutory notices in January, representations in January–February, and review in February–March. The actual notice will set the representation dates.

    Leaflet, PDF page 7 · General FAQ

  4. PLANNED · April 2027

    Final determination

    The published timetable places the committee’s final decision in April. If closure is approved on the proposed timetable, it would take effect on 1 September 2027.

    Leaflet timetable · Current proposal

Why we use 16 October: the specific response form gives this exact date. The leaflet describes the initial period as September–November, while the general FAQ says it ends in October. The leaflet’s later review periods also overlap its planned November committee stage. Check official updates before acting; no closing time or statutory deadline is inferred here.

If closure is approved

The council proposes a place at Darell Primary for every current Kew Riverside pupil, with the option to apply to other schools with vacancies. Detailed pupil transition arrangements still matter. Read the council’s offer.

If closure does not proceed

The proposed closure date would not be an instruction to close. The council would need to confirm the next steps. An alternative would need agreed delivery, funding and pupil assumptions; this collection does not establish that any alternative is approved or viable.

02 / Public responsibilities

Who will decide Kew Riverside’s future?

Start with the institution and the decision stage. Being named here does not mean someone supports closure. A person’s office, their public position and their recorded vote are different things.

Richmond’s Education and Children’s Services Committee

The consultation leaflet assigns the decision on proceeding and the final determination to this committee. Its remit includes school place planning. The chair leads committee proceedings. The leaflet assigns these decisions to the committee collectively.

Decision stages · Committee remit (PDF) · Current membership

Committee members

The published remit provides for 11 councillors, two voting diocesan members and a non-voting parent governor representative. Actual attendance, substitutions and voting entitlement must be checked for each meeting. Membership alone reveals no vote.

Council and AfC officers

Achieving for Children delivers children’s services for the council and develops school organisation proposals on its behalf. Officers prepare evidence and administer the process. The FAQ explains AfC’s role.

Representatives and governors

Ward councillors and the MP can represent residents; those roles do not automatically confer a committee vote. The school’s governing body has a collective role. Relevant official statements can be added when verified.

03 / Evidence and open questions

Questions about the proposal

The council explains its proposal through falling rolls and pressure on funding. Its leaflet says reserves will be exhausted by the end of the 2026/27 financial year and forecasts a deficit above £400,000 by 2028/29. These are the council’s projections, not independently audited figures.

Financial rationale, PDF page 3 · Council summary. The questions below are this site’s editorial assessment. “Further detail sought” means detail not located in the sources reviewed here; it does not establish that no assessment exists or that a request has been sent.

What would each realistic alternative cost?
Published explanation · Further detail sought

Council’s published answer: the leaflet considers maintaining current arrangements, reducing intake, shared leadership or federation, amalgamation and academy trust options. It explains why these were not preferred, including continuing financial pressure and implementation complexity.

Still useful to publish: a consistent, costed comparison showing assumptions, delivery partners and conditions under which an alternative could preserve teaching on the site.

Alternatives considered, PDF page 6

What are the net savings after PFI and transition costs?
Financial rationale published · Further detail sought

Available evidence: the council gives a school deficit forecast, and the school describes its private finance initiative arrangement. A deficit forecast is not the same as a calculation of net savings from closure.

Still useful to publish: annual costs that stop, costs that continue, contract obligations, transition costs and any repurposing costs or income. PFI obligations do not by themselves establish that closure saves nothing.

Council’s financial rationale · School’s PFI explanation

What assumptions underpin local pupil forecasts?
Forecasts published · Further detail sought

Council’s published answer: the leaflet includes a Kew Riverside and planning-area series. It reports 87 pupils in 2025/26 and forecasts 84 in 2026/27. The general FAQ says housing developments are taken into account.

Still useful to publish: scheme-specific housing delivery dates, expected pupils by cohort, forecast errors and sensitivity to changed assumptions. Keep borough demand, planning-area totals and school rolls distinct.

Pupil table, PDF page 5 · General FAQ

How will continuity, journeys and SEND support be protected?
Transition commitments published · Further detail sought

Council’s published answer: the Kew consultation page offers every current pupil a Darell place, describes collaboration on transition and commits to tailored support for pupils with special educational needs and disabilities.

Still useful to publish: receiving capacity by year group, continuity and staffing arrangements, safe journey assessments, sibling arrangements and how each support commitment will be delivered. Individual children’s information belongs in appropriate private official channels.

05 / Use the official process

Make your contribution count.

Start with your consultation response.

Respond directly through the official form by the stated date, 16 October 2026. As checked on 21 September, it asks your connection to the school, your view and reasons, and offers space for alternatives and comments. It does not ask for a name or postcode.

The leaflet also gives the institutional email schoolorganisation@achievingforchildren.org.uk and a postal route. Read the official response instructions.

For a relevant committee item, use the council’s speaking and written-question procedures. Check the agenda, eligibility and registration deadlines before applying.

Keep contributions focused on the proposal, evidence and effects on pupils. Respect everyone’s privacy and use official channels for correspondence.
